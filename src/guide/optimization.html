03Linear algebra and optimization

Linear algebra describes exposures, regressions and covariance; optimization turns objectives and limits into allocations or decisions. In a minimum-variance portfolio, for example, the objective is wᵀΣw, where w contains portfolio weights and Σ is the covariance matrix. Budget, leverage and position constraints determine which solutions are feasible. Understanding positive semidefiniteness and conditioning helps explain both the geometry of the problem and why a numerical solution can be sensitive to estimated inputs.

Boyd and Vandenberghe's Convex Optimization is the main text once projections, eigenvalues and quadratic forms are familiar. Begin with convex sets and functions, then constrained formulations and duality. The aim is to recognize the structure of a problem and interpret its optimality conditions before choosing a numerical method. Read the material on convexity and constrained problems before treating a solver as a black box. The authors provide the book freely, along with additional exercises and links to code examples that connect mathematical formulations to computation.

MIT 18.06 should come first if projections, least squares, eigenvalues or positive-definite matrices are still unfamiliar. Use the notes and problems for the specific gaps you need to close. Those topics recur in regression and risk modeling. After reviewing them, solve the same constrained portfolio problem analytically where possible and numerically with a solver. Inspect active constraints and perturb the covariance estimate; the change in weights often teaches more about the problem than the original optimum.
