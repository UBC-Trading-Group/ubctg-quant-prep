07Portfolio construction and systematic research

A return forecast is only one input to an investment decision. You also need to decide how much risk to take, how positions interact and whether the expected return justifies the cost of trading. Separate three questions: what does the signal predict, what evidence supports that prediction, and what portfolio could make use of it? A strong answer to one does not settle the others.

Grinold and Kahn’s Active Portfolio Management provides the investment framework. Its emphasis is performance relative to a benchmark: how forecasts, active risk and portfolio decisions contribute to an information ratio. Read it to understand the relationship between forecasting skill and the opportunities to apply that skill. Examine the assumptions behind the fundamental law of active management; many correlated signals do not provide the same breadth as independent opportunities. Use this book for the investment reasoning, with Palomar below as a freely accessible route into the mathematics.

Before fitting more flexible predictive models, learn how a proposed return relationship is investigated. Bali, Engle and Murray’s Empirical Asset Pricing: The Cross Section of Stock Returns develops portfolio sorts and Fama–MacBeth regressions. Sorts compare subsequent returns across groups; cross-sectional regressions examine relationships while controlling for other characteristics. Work through what each procedure estimates, how observations are weighted and how uncertainty is measured. A return difference alone does not establish whether a characteristic captures risk, mispricing or an effect represented by another variable.

Palomar’s freely available Portfolio Optimization turns an objective into a problem with explicit constraints. Begin with minimum variance, then add weight bounds, turnover limits and return forecasts. Inspect concentration and stability after each change. Study covariance estimation and shrinkage when estimated risk produces unstable weights. For a practical equity research workflow, continue with Coqueret and Guida’s freely available Machine Learning for Factor Investing. Use its code and exercises to follow the path from preprocessing through prediction to portfolio construction, keeping track of when each input became available.

Evaluate the complete investment procedure, including how signals become positions and what rebalancing costs. Keep model selection separate from final evaluation, and record the alternatives tried. A strategy selected from many experiments needs stronger scrutiny than its best backtest suggests. Inspect turnover, concentration and sensitivity to assumptions alongside return.
